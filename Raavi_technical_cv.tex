\section{Independent Research and Software Development}

\cvitem{MoM/MLFMM solver}{Developed a C++ surface-integral-equation solver for perfectly electrically conducting structures, using Rao--Wilton--Glisson (RWG) basis functions and EFIE, MFIE, and CFIE formulations. Accelerated matrix--vector products with the multilevel fast multipole method (MLFMM). Tested problems exceeding one million unknowns and optimized computation time and memory consumption. The solver provides the foundation for the scattering and radiation applications below.}

\cvitem{Antenna radiation}{Developed a hybrid FEM--MoM/MLFMM solver for horn antennas and horn-fed reflectors. Represented the horn interior as a two-port network between the feed waveguide and aperture. A finite-element model supplies its generalized scattering matrix (GSM) and aperture modal fields. Converting the GSM to a modal admittance matrix couples equivalent magnetic currents at the aperture to electric currents on exterior conducting surfaces. Solved the coupled integral equations iteratively with MLFMM acceleration, then evaluated radiation patterns, directivity, gain, and polarization.}

\cvitem{Cavity scattering}{Implemented the hybrid MoM/modal-admittance formulation of Enneking, Ritter, and B\"unger (EuMC 2003) for plane-wave scattering from cavity-backed apertures. Modelled the cavity as a one-port by short-circuiting the far waveguide port. The dense MoM implementation reproduces published radar cross sections for a rectangular cavity; an MLFMM-accelerated implementation is in development.}

\cvitem{Iterative solvers}{Implemented block Jacobi and restricted additive Schwarz preconditioners, including local Schur complements compressed with rank-revealing QR; integrated hierarchical-LU preconditioning through HLIBpro. Developed block GMRES to solve multiple right-hand sides simultaneously, including different incidence angles and polarizations. Used Intel oneMKL and OpenMP for numerical performance.}

\cvitem{$\mathcal{H}^2$ methods}{Experimental $\mathcal{H}^2$-matrix approach to the same surface integral equations, investigated as an alternative to MLFMM for far-field interactions, with attention to accuracy, execution time, and memory requirements.}
